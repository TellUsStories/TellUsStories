60 questions to get to know your grandparents’ life (and your mum’s and dad’s)

One day we realise we know almost nothing about our grandparents’ lives: what they were like as children, what they dreamt of, how they fell in love. And the sooner you ask, the more is kept. These questions work for grandparents, but also for your mum, your dad or any older member of the family. They are ordered by stage of life, from childhood to today.

You don’t need to ask them all, or all at once. Choose the ones you like best, let the conversation flow and, above all, record the answers: their voice and their way of telling it are worth as much as the story itself.

Childhood: what they were like as a child

  1. Where were you born and what was the house you grew up in like?
  2. How many brothers and sisters were there, and where did you come in the family?
  3. What did you play with your friends? What was your favourite toy?
  4. What is your very first memory?
  5. What bit of mischief did you get up to that you still remember?
  6. What was your school like? Which subject did you love and which did you hate?
  7. What food from your childhood do you miss?
  8. How did you celebrate birthdays and Christmas?
  9. What did you want to be when you grew up?
  10. Who was the person who looked after you most or made you laugh?

Youth: dreams and adventures

  1. What were you like at fifteen or twenty? Shy, rebellious, adventurous?
  2. What music did you listen to and where did you go with your friends?
  3. What was your first job and how much did you earn?
  4. Did you study? Why did you choose that path?
  5. What was the most exciting trip of your youth?
  6. What brave decision did you make that changed your life?
  7. What was your biggest dream back then? Did it come true?
  8. What did you do in your free time?
  9. Who was your best friend and what did you do together?
  10. What is the craziest thing you have ever done?

Love: how it all began

  1. How did you meet grandad or grandma?
  2. What was the first thing that caught your eye?
  3. What was your first date like?
  4. How did you tell each other how you felt, or what was the proposal like?
  5. What was your wedding like, or the day you decided to live together?
  6. Where did you live at first and what was your first home like?
  7. What song reminds you of that time?
  8. What was the hardest moment you got through together?
  9. What advice would you give about love?
  10. What do you love most about your partner, even today?

Family: the arrival of each child

  1. How did you feel when you found out you were going to be a mum or a dad?
  2. What was the day each of your children was born like?
  3. What were your children like when they were little? What mischief did they get up to?
  4. What family traditions did you have?
  5. What were your favourite family outings?
  6. What was the hardest thing about bringing up your children?
  7. And the most beautiful?
  8. What did you do for a living while your children were growing up?
  9. What are you proudest of about your family?
  10. How did you feel when your first grandchild was born?

Today: with the grandchildren

  1. What do you most like doing with your grandchildren?
  2. What food do you make them that they love?
  3. What is special about each of your grandchildren?
  4. What game or habit do only you share?
  5. What have your grandchildren taught you?
  6. What is a normal day in your life like now?
  7. What hobbies do you have today?
  8. What makes you laugh the most?
  9. What has changed the most since you were little?
  10. What plans would you still like to do?

Legacy: what they want the family to know

  1. What is the most important thing you have learnt in life?
  2. What would you like your grandchildren to remember about you?
  3. What advice would you give them for when they grow up?
  4. Which decision are you happiest about?
  5. Is there anything you would do differently?
  6. What values would you like to live on in the family?
  7. What recipe, object or story would you like to be passed down from generation to generation?
  8. What makes you proud of yourself?
  9. What would you say to your grandchildren if they could read you twenty years from now?
  10. If your life were a book, what would it be called?

Tips to keep the conversation flowing

  • Do it in several sittings. Ten minutes every now and then is better than a whole afternoon: they get less tired and more memories come out.
  • Record it on your phone. A voice note is enough. Their voice telling their memories is a treasure in itself.
  • Bring old photos. A photo brings back memories that a question on its own cannot.
  • Ask for details. “And what did that house smell like?”, “what were you wearing?”: the little details are what make a story beautiful.
  • Let them go off script. If one question leads them to another story, follow it.
  • Include the grandchildren. Let them ask a question or two: they love discovering that their grandparents were children once too.
