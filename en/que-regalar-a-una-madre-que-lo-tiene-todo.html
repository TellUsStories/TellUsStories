What to give a mum who has everything

Her house is full, she says “don’t buy me anything” and every year the same dilemma comes round. The good news is that a mum who has everything almost never has what would truly delight her, because that isn’t sold in any shop. Here is how to think about it, 10 ideas that won’t end up in a drawer and the mistakes worth avoiding. It works for her birthday, Mother’s Day or any other occasion.

What she says and what she means

Before looking for ideas, some translation is needed. These phrases come up in almost every home:

  • Don’t buy me anything.I don’t want more things to put away. I want you to think about me.
  • I already have everything.I’ve got the material things covered. What I’m missing is time with you.
  • Just you coming is enough.She means it. A whole afternoon is worth more than a rushed visit.
  • Don’t spend your money.I don’t want you to spend out of obligation. A thoughtful gift beats an expensive one.

How to get it right for a woman who has everything

Four quick questions before you buy anything:

  1. Will she use it or put it away? If the answer is “put it away”, she already has a cupboard full of those.
  2. Does it say something about her? If you could just as easily give it to a work colleague, it isn’t her gift.
  3. Does it save her work or make more? A delicate plant or a gadget with 40 functions are chores too.
  4. Will she remember it in ten years? Things are forgotten. Moments and stories are not.

10 ideas for a mum who has everything (that won’t take up a drawer)

To give her back some time

1. Fix what has been waiting for years. The dripping tap, the pictures to hang, the blind that sticks. Give her a card that says “we’re doing it this Saturday” and keep your word.

2. An afternoon of help with her phone. Sorting her photos, making a backup, deleting what she doesn’t need and showing her that trick she always asks you about. Patiently and without watching the clock.

To give her back memories

3. Digitise the video tapes or slides. Many families have boxes of tapes that nobody can watch any more. There are services that convert them to digital for very little. The real gift is the afternoon you spend watching them together.

4. The music from when she was 20. Casually ask her which record she played on repeat and find it on vinyl, or see if that band is still touring.

5. Reconnect her with someone from her past. That friend from school or from her first job she lost touch with. With social media, sometimes a couple of messages is all it takes. A coffee together could be the best gift of the year.

To surprise her

6. A treat she never allows herself. Flowers every month, a magazine she likes, lessons in that thing she has always said she would do “one day”. What she would buy if she thought a little more about herself.

7. Ask her to teach you her speciality. Her tortilla, her way of knitting, her trick for keeping plants alive. The gift is your interest: almost every mum loves teaching what she knows how to do.

8. A regular date in the calendar. A breakfast once a month, just the two of you, booked in for the whole year. Mums who have everything often have a diary empty of plans with their children.

To last

9. A family time capsule. Each child and grandchild puts something in: a letter, a drawing, a photo. It is sealed with a date and opened as a family five years later.

10. Her story, told for the children. What she was like as a little girl, what she wanted to be, how she met her partner. Many of these stories only she knows, and the grandchildren (or her children) have never heard them in full. You can record her telling them or turn them into a book.

If she is turning 60, 70 or 80: what to give her

Age changes a lot about what moves people. A clue for each stage:

  • At 60: a stage with more free time often begins. Experiences and learning something new work well.
  • At 70: gifts that bring the family together win, like a lunch with everyone or a short group trip.
  • At 80: memory matters most. Recording her voice, sorting her photos with her or telling her life in a book.

Three mistakes that happen every year

  • Giving what you would like yourself. It happens more than you would think. Think about her tastes, not yours.
  • Buying “something for the house”. The house isn’t her. A new frying pan is useful, but it doesn’t move anyone.
  • Leaving it to the last day. The best ideas on this list need time: tracking down a friend, digitising tapes or making a made-to-order book, which takes 2 or 3 weeks.

Frequently asked questions

What do you give a mum who has everything?

What you can’t buy in a shop: time together, memories brought back, something that saves her work or a gift that speaks about her life. A good test: if the gift could work for anyone else, it probably isn’t hers.

What original gift can I give my mum that isn’t an object?

Fixing something that has been waiting for years, digitising her video tapes and watching them together, finding a childhood friend she lost touch with or booking a regular monthly date just for the two of you.

What to give a mum for her 70th or 80th birthday?

From 70 onwards, the gifts that tend to move her most are the ones that bring the family together and preserve her memories: gathering everyone for a lunch, recording her voice telling stories or turning her life into a book for the grandchildren.

How much should you spend on a gift for a mum who has everything?

You don’t need to spend much. Some of the best ideas are free, like fixing something at home or asking her to teach you her speciality. What counts is that the gift shows you know her.
