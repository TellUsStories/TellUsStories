What to give a man who has everything

If he needs something, he buys it himself the same day. It happens with lots of dads, grandads, partners and uncles: they already have the good tool, the watch and their team’s sweatshirt. This guide is not another list of gadgets. It is a simple method for finding what he doesn’t have, ideas based on his personality, and what to give him if he is an older man or if it is his birthday.

Why it is so hard to find him a gift

Three things almost always come together:

  • He buys things himself. He doesn’t wait for his birthday to treat himself.
  • He gives no clues. He answers “nothing, I don’t need anything” and he means it.
  • He knows more than you about his own interests. If he loves fishing, wine or DIY, it is hard to surprise him with something he doesn’t already know.

That is why it works better to stop looking for objects and look in three different places instead: his time, his hobbies and his past.

The three-list trick (before you buy anything)

Take a piece of paper and spend five minutes writing down:

  1. What he does when nobody asks anything of him. That is where his real hobbies are, not the ones you think he has.
  2. What he says he will do “one day” and never does. A trip, a course, picking up the guitar again.
  3. What he always talks about at family meals. His military service, his first car, summers in the village. That story he keeps repeating is a huge clue.

The best gift usually comes from crossing two of the lists.

Example: he loves cooking (list 1) and always talks about his mother’s stews (list 3). Gift: an afternoon together cooking that recipe, with the ingredients already bought.

Ideas based on what he is like

  • The handymanHe has every tool. Give him a project: the materials and an afternoon to make something specific together, like restoring a family chair or building a birdhouse with the grandchildren.
  • The one who never sits stillTickets to watch his sport live, a route you have planned with a stop for lunch, or signing up together for a charity walk.
  • The family cookA one-day course in something he hasn’t mastered (bread, rice dishes, smoking), a hard-to-find product from his home region, or a recipe book of his dishes written by the whole family.
  • The nostalgic oneThe record, film or comic from when he was 15, an old photo of his neighbourhood enlarged and framed, or a model of the car from his youth.
  • The tech loverHe already buys himself every gadget. Better to give a service: digitising his old photos, making a video from them or being his “tech support” for a whole day.
  • The man of few wordsShowy gifts are not his thing. A simple plan that repeats is enough: a breakfast once a month, a match together or a regular game of cards.

Gifts that get used up (so they never get in the way)

People who have everything often have little space left too. Consumable gifts avoid that problem:

  • Food or drink he wouldn’t buy himself. A good cured ham, olive oil from a small mill or coffee from a roaster in his town.
  • A subscription to something he loves. A magazine about his hobby, a monthly box of craft beers or a book club.
  • An experience with a date. A tasting, a night away, a flight in a light aircraft. With a fixed date, because “whenever you like” vouchers go unused.

What to give an older man who has everything

From a certain age, what he values most is not something new, but that his life isn’t lost. Three ideas that work:

  • Record him telling his life story. A phone is enough: an afternoon, a coffee and a few questions. These questions to get to know your grandparents’ life work just as well for a dad or an uncle.
  • Go back to a place from his life. His village, the street where he grew up or the football pitch in his neighbourhood. With company and no rush.
  • Tell his story for the grandchildren. What he was like as a child, what work he did, how he met his partner. It can be an album, a recording or an illustrated storybook.

A trick to get him talking

Many men open up more when they are doing something side by side with someone than when they are sitting face to face. Ask him in the car, while you are cooking or out on a walk. The best stories usually come out like that, almost without meaning to.

If it is for his birthday: think about his year

For a man who has everything, the birthday number gives you a lot to play with. At 50, 60 or 70, gifts “from his year” work well: the newspaper from the day he was born, the number one song that week or a lunch with the dishes from his childhood. If it is a big birthday, the best gift is to bring together the people he loves most. What he is sure to have already

If in doubt, these are almost always one too many:

  • Wallet or belt number five.
  • Whisky stones (and the matching glasses).
  • The T-shirt that says “world’s best dad”.
  • The gadget from a TV advert that gets used once.
  • An aftershave “just in case”, without knowing which one he wears.

They are not bad gifts. He just has them already. On the blog there are more guides to getting it right, such as what to give a mum who has everything.

Frequently asked questions

What do you give a man who has everything?

Something he can’t buy himself: a plan with a date, a project to do together, something linked to his past or his own story told for the family. To get it right, look at what he does in his free time and the stories he keeps telling.

What can I give my dad, who has everything?

Time together and memories: a live match, cooking his trusty recipe with him, digitising his old photos or recording him telling his life story for the grandchildren.

What to give an older man who has everything?

From 70 onwards, the gifts that tend to move them are the ones that preserve their memories: going back to a place from their youth, sorting out their photos with them or turning their story into a book or a recording for the family.

What birthday present can you give a man who has everything?

One linked to his age or the year he was born: the newspaper from the day he was born, the music from that year or a lunch with the dishes from his childhood. If it is a big birthday, bring together the people he loves most.
