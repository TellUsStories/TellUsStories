Dedications for giving a book: lovely ideas

A book given as a gift is kept for years, and often what gets reread most is not the book but the dedication. A few lines written by hand (or printed in a personalised book) turn a gift into a keepsake. Here are ideas for mum, dad, the grandparents and the children, and a few tips for writing your own.

How to write a good dedication

  • Start with their name or nickname. “For Mari” is more moving than “For you”.
  • Include a detail only the two of you understand: a song, a trip, something they always say.
  • Say why you are giving them this book and not another one.
  • Add the date and the place. Twenty years from now, that’s what will matter most.
  • Sign the way they call you. “Your daughter”, “your grandchildren”, “the baby of the family”.

Dedications for mum

  • “Mum: this book tells your story, but you wrote ours.”
  • “For the woman who taught us to count to three… and never actually got to three.”
  • “Before you were a mum, you were so many things. Thank you for letting us get to know them.”
  • “So you never forget how much we admire you, even if we don’t say it often enough.”

Dedications for dad

  • “Dad: thank you for all the bedtime stories. Now it’s your turn to hear yours.”
  • “For the one who always had a solution, a bad joke and time for us.”
  • “So your grandchildren know that, before you were a grandad, you were a boy with lots of dreams.”

Dedications for grandparents

  • “Grandma: your stories are our greatest treasure. Here they are, kept safe for ever.”
  • “For the grandad who taught us that life is best told without rushing.”
  • “So that those who come after us know where we come from. Thank you for everything, Grandma and Grandad.”

Dedications for children

  • “So you know where you come from and how much we love you.”
  • “This book is yours. Read it today, and read it again when you’re grown up.”
  • “For Lucas and Martina: this is your family’s story. Now it’s your turn to write yours.”

Short dedications

  • “For you, because you are part of this story.”
  • “With everything that doesn’t fit in a dedication.”
  • “To read together. Today and always.”

Funny dedications

  • “Spoiler: at the end of the book, we still love you.”
  • “So you can’t say we only ever give you socks.”
  • “Lending it out is forbidden. Well, except to the grandchildren.”

Frequently asked questions

Where do you write the dedication in a book?

On the first blank page, before the title or just after it. If the book is personalised, the dedication is usually printed on one of the first pages.

How long should a dedication be?

One to four sentences. What matters is not the length, but that it clearly feels written for that person: a memory, a nickname or a phrase that only the two of you understand.

Should I put the date in the dedication?

It’s highly recommended. Twenty years from now, the date and the place turn the book into a memory of that moment: “Christmas 2026, at Grandma and Grandad’s”.

What should I write if the book is for a young child?

Simple sentences they can understand now and that will move them when they grow up. For example: “So you know where you come from and how much we love you”.
